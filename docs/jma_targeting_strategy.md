## Working Manuscript Title

**Semantic Gap, Scenario Lock-In, and Trust Response in Sunscreen Consumer Reviews: A Feasibility Study Using Amazon Reviews 2023**

This title is preferred over a theory-heavy title such as *Commercial Semantic Drift and Commodity Potential Attenuation* because it foregrounds a clear applied marketing analytics problem:

- consumer review analytics;
- product semantic mismatch;
- trust response;
- scenario lock-in;
- sunscreen/SPF products as the empirical case.

The broader concept of commodity potential attenuation can appear in the introduction and discussion, but it should not carry the title or abstract in the first JMA-facing version.

## Three-Part Contribution

1. **Method contribution**  
   The paper develops a reproducible review-analytics pipeline for measuring semantic gap, trust response, scenario lock-in, and experiential complaint signals in consumer review data.

2. **Empirical contribution**  
   The paper tests this pipeline on 100 sunscreen/SPF products and 50,000 reviews from Amazon Reviews 2023, producing a product-month panel with 9,124 observations.

3. **Substantive contribution**  
   The paper finds that semantic gap is more consistently associated with scenario lock-in than direct skepticism. Direct negative response is concentrated around concrete experiential failures, especially white cast, texture/greasiness, and perceived burn failure among higher-SPF products.

## Recommended Framing

Recommended framing:

> This study develops and tests a reproducible marketing analytics pipeline for measuring how semantic mismatch in consumer-facing product discourse is associated with trust response, scenario lock-in, and experiential complaint signals in sunscreen reviews.

Avoid overclaiming:

> This study proves that commercial semantic drift causes commodity potential attenuation.

The current evidence is best presented as a feasibility study with strong applied analytics value and a clear route to stronger longitudinal designs.

## JMA Fit

This project fits JMA because it combines:

- public large-scale consumer review data;
- reproducible text and panel analytics;
- product-level marketing strategy implications;
- applied managerial insights for claim monitoring and review-based early warning;
- an explicit data-quality and measurement-validity orientation.

The most JMA-compatible vocabulary is:

- review analytics;
- product semantics;
- semantic mismatch;
- trust response;
- scenario lock-in;
- experiential complaint signals;
- applied marketing analytics pipeline.

Use the term **commodity potential attenuation** carefully as an interpretive extension, not as the primary empirical claim.
